Edge-aware Graph Refinement (EGR) encodes geometric relations among neighboring local regions, while Cluster-Deviation Modeling (CDM) measures each region relative to structurally similar peers to produce discriminative anomaly scores.

Abstract

3D anomaly detection identifies defective regions in point clouds, but most existing methods are normality-centered: they learn normal data distributions and treat deviations as anomalies. This can create ambiguous decision boundaries, especially in unified and cross-domain settings. We propose GRIM, a relational inconsistency modeling framework that instead characterizes defects as violations of geometric consistency among neighboring structures. GRIM learns category-agnostic defect cues from controlled pseudo-anomalies and contains two main components: Edge-aware Graph Refinement (EGR) for relation-aware local feature learning and Cluster-Deviation Modeling (CDM) for identifying regions that are incompatible with their structural peer groups. Experiments on Anomaly-ShapeNet and Real3D-AD show consistent improvements in both in-domain and cross-domain settings.

Why Relational Inconsistency?

Reconstruction- and feature-similarity-based 3D anomaly detectors usually decide abnormality from deviation relative to learned normal distributions. However, a rare but valid normal structure can be far from that distribution, while a real defect can still appear locally similar to normal regions. These two cases lead to false positives and false negatives, and the problem becomes harder when a single model must handle many categories or a previously unseen domain.

Key idea. GRIM does not try to enumerate every possible real-world defect. Instead, it uses controlled pseudo-anomalies to teach the model a transferable criterion: a defect breaks geometric relations that should remain consistent among neighboring or structurally similar regions.

Method

During training, GRIM starts from normal point clouds and synthesizes localized bulge, sink, and hole perturbations. These pseudo-anomalies are not intended to reproduce the full appearance distribution of real defects; they serve as controlled violations of local geometric consistency. The resulting point-level masks provide explicit supervision for learning a defect criterion.

1. Edge-aware Graph Refinement (EGR)

Each point cloud is partitioned into local groups and represented as a graph. Node features encode local regions, while edges describe relative centroid displacement, point-spread differences, normal inconsistency, and curvature differences. Relation-aware message passing then refines each node using the geometry of its neighbors, allowing the representation to capture structural compatibility rather than only isolated local appearance.

2. Cluster-Deviation Modeling (CDM)

EGR-refined node features are grouped into clusters of structurally similar regions. GRIM measures how far each region deviates from its assigned cluster center and injects this cluster-relative deviation into the anomaly classifier. A deviation loss further encourages pseudo-anomalous nodes to separate from their structural peers, providing a finer-grained criterion that transfers across categories and domains.
